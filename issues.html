The Clean Mobility Question

A closer look at lifecycle, access, energy security, and travel demand.

01 Problem Focus

The core problem is evaluating whether Electric Vehicles (EVs) and Automated Vehicles (AVs) truly deliver clean, sustainable, and equitable mobility when their full operational lifecycle, economic barriers, and societal trade-offs are considered.

The EV trade-off: Energy & Emissions vs. Access

Fuel and emissions savings: EVs eliminate tailpipe exhaust and reduce exposure to global oil-price swings (Recharged, n.d.).
Hidden costs and grid limits: Footprints shift to power generation where grids rely on fossil fuels (Earth.Org, 2022). Battery mineral extraction adds ecological strain (Yucel, 2025), and battery replacement is costly (Babylon2k, 2025).

The AV trade-off: Efficiency vs. Increased Travel Demand

Efficiency gains: Smoother acceleration and smarter routing reduce fuel burn and congestion (Earth.Org, 2022).
Rebound and computing load: Onboard computing raises energy use by 3–20%, and easier commuting can increase total miles (Earth.Org, 2022).

Challenges Ahead for AVs

CostFully self-driving cars are expensive, though prices are expected to drop (UCF Online, 2025).
Safety and ethicsCars must make split-second decisions, raising questions about who is protected (UCF Online, 2025).
RegulationsLaws on AVs are still being developed and vary by region (UCF Online, 2025).
WeatherSnow, rain, and fog can affect sensors and AI decisions (UCF Online, 2025).
TrustAccidents involving self-driving cars have made some people cautious (UCF Online, 2025).
FairnessBenefits may not reach low-income and marginalized communities unless equity is planned in (Yucel, 2025).

The Bigger Picture: Philippines

A cleaner future is possible if we plan for it. EVs and AVs can help reduce emissions, improve transport efficiency, and build more livable cities, but the transition needs the right policies, infrastructure, and public support.
